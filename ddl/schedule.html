Depth, width & optimization

Week 01Presenter: TBD

Depth changes learning dynamics

What does adding layers change, even when the resulting function is still linear?

Foundations

Saxe, McClelland & Ganguli, Exact Solutions to the Nonlinear Dynamics of Learning in Deep Linear Neural Networks (2014). Matrix factorization, singular modes, and gradient flow.

Recent main reading & follow-up

Bordelon & Pehlevan, Deep Linear Network Training Dynamics from Random Initialization: Data, Width, Depth, and Hyperparameter Transfer (2025). Focus on how parameterization and depth change learning dynamics, and what these models explain about scaling and hyperparameter transfer. This session is mathematically demanding; focus on one tractable setting from the paper.

Week 02Presenter: TBD

NTK and feature learning

When is a neural network effectively a kernel machine, and when does it learn its own representation?

Foundations

Jacot et al., Neural Tangent Kernel (2018). Selected ideas from Yang & Hu, Feature Learning in Infinite-Width Neural Networks (2021).

Recent main reading & follow-up

Haas et al., On the Surprising Effectiveness of Large Learning Rates under Standard Width Scaling (2025). A useful contemporary challenge to an oversimplified reading of NTK: under cross-entropy loss, certain large-learning-rate regimes retain feature learning even in an infinite-width limit. The session should make the assumptions behind the different limits explicit.

Week 03Presenter: TBD

The geometry of optimization: why Muon?

What does “steepest descent” mean when a parameter is a matrix?

Foundations

SVD, polar decomposition, and steepest descent under different norms. Selected background from Shampoo (2018).

Recent main reading & follow-up

Liu et al., Muon Is Scalable for LLM Training (2025), with Bernstein & Newhouse, Old Optimizer, New Norm: An Anthology (2024), as the mathematical companion. The interesting object is the geometry of the update: what changes when we normalize a matrix through its singular directions?

Part 02

Computation & memory

Week 04Presenter: TBD

Hypernetworks: networks that generate networks

Can adapting a model become a learned forward pass?

Foundations

Ha, Dai & Le, HyperNetworks (2016/17). Conditional parameter generation and amortized computation.

Recent main reading & follow-up

Charakorn et al., Text-to-LoRA: Instant Transformer Adaption (2025), followed by Doc-to-LoRA: Learning to Instantly Internalize Contexts (2026). The progression is especially nice: generate weights from a task description, then generate adapters that internalize a document or context.

Week 05Presenter: TBD

Attention, recurrence, and state-space duality

When are apparently different sequence models alternative computations of the same structured operator?

Foundations

Katharopoulos et al., Transformers Are RNNs (2020). Linear attention, state-space recurrences, and matrix associativity.

Recent main reading & follow-up

Dao & Gu, Transformers Are SSMs: Generalized Models and Efficient Algorithms Through Structured State Space Duality (2024). Present the structured-matrix connection underlying Mamba-2. The mathematical content is the correspondence between specified forms of attention and state-space computation, including how it enables different parallel and recurrent implementations.

Week 06Presenter: TBD

Learning as a memory mechanism

What if the recurrent hidden state is itself a model that learns?

Foundations

Ba et al., Using Fast Weights to Attend to the Recent Past (2016). Associative memory and fast versus slow weights.

Recent main reading & follow-up

Sun et al., Learning to (Learn at Test Time): RNNs with Expressive Hidden States (2024), with Titans: Learning to Memorize at Test Time (2024/25) as the follow-up. Focus on how an online learning rule becomes a recurrent update, and what forgetting and error correction contribute.

Part 03

Representations

Week 07Presenter: TBD

Oscillatory representations and synchronization

Could relationships between entities be represented through synchronized dynamics?

Foundations

The Kuramoto model, coupled oscillators, synchronization, and Hopfield-style dynamical networks.

Recent main reading & follow-up

Miyato, Löwe, Geiger & Welling, Artificial Kuramoto Oscillatory Neurons (2024; ICLR 2025). A particularly good fit for the course: it uses interacting oscillatory neurons to explore binding and representation. The presentation can connect a classical dynamical system to modern object discovery and reasoning experiments.

Week 08Presenter: TBD

The geometry of self-supervised representations

What distribution should embeddings have, and how can we encourage it?

Foundations

Bardes et al., VICReg (2021/22). Collapse, variance, covariance, and distribution matching.

Recent main reading & follow-up

Balestriero & LeCun, LeJEPA: Provable and Scalable Self-Supervised Learning Without the Heuristics (2025). Study the motivation for an isotropic Gaussian embedding distribution and the SIGReg objective. A useful discussion is which downstream assumptions justify the desired representation geometry.

Week 09Presenter: TBD

Do different models learn the same representation?

What would “the same” actually mean?

Foundations

Kornblith et al., Similarity of Neural Network Representations Revisited (2019): CKA, invariances, and representation comparison.

Recent main reading & follow-up

Read Huh et al., The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (2024), then center the discussion on Gröger et al., Revisiting the Platonic Representation Hypothesis: An Aristotelian View (2026). The newer paper examines scale-dependent confounds in similarity metrics and finds a more qualified picture, with local neighborhood agreement surviving calibration more convincingly than global agreement.

Part 04

Mechanistic interpretability

Week 10Presenter: TBD

Mechanistic interpretability I: superposition and features

Are the meaningful units neurons, directions, or a chosen decomposition?

Foundations

Sparse coding and dictionary learning; Elhage et al., Toy Models of Superposition (2022).

Recent main reading & follow-up

Templeton et al., Scaling Monosemanticity: Extracting Interpretable Features from Claude 3 Sonnet (2024), followed by Sparse Autoencoders Do Not Find Canonical Units of Analysis (2025). The mathematical thread is overcomplete representation, sparsity, and identifiability: an interpretable dictionary need not be a unique decomposition of the network.

Week 11Presenter: TBD

Mechanistic interpretability II: circuits and causal explanations

How do we move from identifying features to explaining a computation?

Foundations

Selected sections of A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits (2021). Residual streams, attention circuits, and interventions.

Recent main reading & follow-up

Ameisen et al., Circuit Tracing: Revealing Computational Graphs in Language Models (2025), followed by Arora et al., Language Model Circuits Are Sparse in the Neuron Basis (2026). Compare feature-based computational graphs with direct analysis in the neuron basis, and examine how interventions validate a proposed circuit.

Part 05

Generative modeling

Week 12Presenter: TBD

Why does diffusion generalize instead of memorizing?

Where can novelty come from when the empirical training distribution is finite?

Foundations

Vincent, A Connection Between Score Matching and Denoising Autoencoders (2011). Denoising as conditional expectation and its connection to the score.

Recent main reading & follow-up

Kamb & Ganguli, An Analytic Theory of Creativity in Convolutional Diffusion Models (2025). The paper develops an analytic account of how locality and equivariance can produce recombination and generalization in convolutional diffusion models. Keep the scope of the explanation tied to the models studied.

Slides / notes: TBD Week 12 ↗
Week 13Presenter: TBD

From instantaneous velocity to average flow

What must a generative model learn to traverse an entire time interval in one step?

Foundations

Lipman et al., Flow Matching for Generative Modeling (2022/23). Probability paths, conditional vector fields, and ODEs.

Recent main reading & follow-up

Geng et al., Mean Flows for One-step Generative Modeling (2025), with Improved Mean Flows: On the Challenges of Fastforward Generative Models (2025; revised 2026) as the follow-up. Center the session on the relation between instantaneous and interval-averaged velocity, then explain why that relation creates a nontrivial training problem.
