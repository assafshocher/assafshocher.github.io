Topics & readings

Learn the foundations, understand a central paper, examine what came next.

These 23 topics are candidates for the seminar. Only the topics students and the instructor choose will enter the final 13-week schedule. Selection, order and presenter assignments are TBD.

Suggested selection priorities for this graduate seminar, balancing major conceptual gaps, mathematical substance, accessible foundations, current research and breadth across deep learning. The first 13 form a provisional shortlist. Topic numbers remain stable; weekly selections, teaching order and presenters are TBD. Selection priority is shown separately from the stable topic number, so previous references keep their meaning.

Each selected topic gets two academic hours and one focused question, with one reading bundle. The main paper is the center; background and recent developments supply the context and discussion. Topic numbers identify candidates and are independent of the weekly schedule.

All students complete one required shared pre-reading, chosen by the presenters in consultation with the instructor and announced one week before class. Allow 30–45 minutes for a tutorial, a short paper or specified sections. Presenters include two guiding questions; each student brings one question or point of confusion. The main paper is optional for the audience unless selected as the shared pre-reading. Specific pre-readings are TBD.

The reading bundles connect foundational ideas to recent work. Use the scope and presentation focus to decide which sections to develop carefully in class. Supplementary papers support that question; every linked paper need not become a full presentation. Flow matching is assumed background from the base course.

Selection priority 1 · Topic 01Two academic hours

NTK and feature learning

Kernels, feature learning & optimization

A foundational framework with a clean function-space derivation and a precise contrast between kernel behavior and feature learning.

When can a neural network be understood as a kernel method, and when do its features change?

Background & foundations

Lee et al., Wide Neural Networks of Any Depth Evolve as Linear Models Under Gradient Descent (2019), selected sections on Taylor linearization and squared-loss dynamics. Review Jacobians, Gram matrices and gradient flow.

Main paper

Jacot, Gabriel & Hongler, Neural Tangent Kernel: Convergence and Generalization in Neural Networks (2018).

Recent development & discussion

Haas et al., On the Surprising Effectiveness of Large Learning Rates under Standard Width Scaling (2025).

Presentation focus

Derive function-space gradient flow and its solution for squared loss, then compare a finite network with its linearization. Use the recent paper to examine why width, parameterization, learning rate and the choice of loss matter for claims about feature learning.

Selection priority 2 · Topic 08Two academic hours

Test-time training (TTT)

Sequence models & reasoning

A major change in how we view inference: explicit learning becomes part of the recurrent computation, with a concrete long-context application.

What should a model learn at inference, and which objective makes those updates useful?

Scope for one session

Two academic hours: develop a recurrent TTT update, then examine end-to-end long-context training. Original distribution-shift TTT supplies the motivation and history; IT³ is supplementary reading.

Background & foundations

Sun, Wang, Liu, Miller, Efros & Hardt, Test-Time Training with Self-Supervision for Generalization under Distribution Shifts (ICML 2020), selected introduction and auxiliary-task formulation. Ba et al., Using Fast Weights to Attend to the Recent Past (2016), fast versus slow weights and associative memory.

Main paper

Sun et al., Learning to (Learn at Test Time): RNNs with Expressive Hidden States (2024). A model is the recurrent hidden state, and self-supervised gradient updates are the state transition.

Recent development & discussion

Tandon et al., End-to-End Test-Time Training for Long Context (2025). Next-token updates compress context into weights, with a meta-learned initialization. Supplementary adaptation example: Durasov, Shocher, Oner, Chechik, Efros & Fua, IT³: Idempotent Test-Time Training (ICML 2025); implementation.

Presentation focus

Start with the original 2020 adaptation problem, then work through a TTT-Linear update and its recurrent computation. Examine how end-to-end next-token training compresses context into weights, and explain the training and inference costs. IT³ provides a supplementary consistency-based adaptation example with its own training setup.

Sequence models & reasoning

Selection priority 4 · Topic 16Two academic hours

Mechanistic interpretability: from features to circuits

Weight space & interpretability

A distinctive way to study networks scientifically, connecting superposition to a causal circuit experiment rather than only inspecting correlations.

How do interpretable features support a circuit explanation, and how can we test that explanation causally?

Scope for one session

Two academic hours: introduce superposition and sparse features, then develop one circuit-tracing case study and causal intervention.

Background & foundations

Elhage et al., Toy Models of Superposition (2022), one small example of sparse features sharing dimensions. Selected background: Elhage et al., A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits (2021), the residual stream; Templeton et al., Scaling Monosemanticity: Extracting Interpretable Features from Claude 3 Sonnet (2024), the sparse-autoencoder objective and one feature illustration.

Main paper

Ameisen et al., Circuit Tracing: Revealing Computational Graphs in Language Models (2025).

Recent development & discussion

Kamath et al., Tracing Attention Computation Through Feature Interactions (2025). Limitation reading: Leask et al., Sparse Autoencoders Do Not Find Canonical Units of Analysis (2025), a caution about treating extracted features as canonical units.

Presentation focus

Use a toy example to explain superposition and introduce sparse feature extraction briefly. Center the presentation on how a replacement model produces an attribution graph, one circuit case study and a causal intervention. Distinguish sparse autoencoders from the tracing method’s replacement model. Assess omitted computations and approximation error; interpreting a feature and validating a circuit are different steps.

Selection priority 5 · Topic 14Two academic hours

Hypernetworks and learning in weight space

Weight space & interpretability

Treating networks themselves as data opens a different viewpoint; hidden-neuron symmetries supply a substantial mathematical core.

How can a neural network generate, read or edit another network's weights while respecting their symmetries?

Scope for one session

One seminar of two academic hours. The core is permutation symmetry and one weight-space expressivity result. HyperNetworks supplies the generation idea; SHINE supplies one current application. Doc-to-LoRA is supporting reading.

Background & foundations

Ha, Dai & Le, HyperNetworks (ICLR 2017), a network generating another network's parameters. Navon, Shamsian, Achituve, Fetaya, Chechik & Maron, Equivariant Architectures for Learning in Deep Weight Spaces (ICML 2023), selected permutation action and DWS-layer construction; the authors' tutorial is an accessible introduction. Hu et al., LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models (2021/22), selected low-rank update formulation.

Main paper

Dayan, Eitan & Maron, On the Expressive Power of Permutation-Equivariant Weight-Space Networks (ICML 2026). Focus on the approximation framework and one universality result for fixed-architecture MLP weight spaces: distinguish properties of parameterizations from properties of the functions those parameters realize.

Recent development & discussion

Liu, Wang, Mao, Gelberg, Maron & Zhang, SHINE: A Scalable In-Context Hypernetwork for Mapping Context to LoRA in a Single Pass (ICML 2026), a contemporary application that converts context into adapters. Supporting application reading: Charakorn et al., Doc-to-LoRA: Learning to Instantly Internalize Contexts (ICML 2026), amortized context distillation.

Presentation focus

Derive the hidden-neuron permutation action for a small MLP and construct a simple equivariant weight-processing layer. Explain why arbitrary flattening obscures this structure. Work through one proof sketch connecting canonicalization to universality under a general-position assumption, then inspect a degenerate counterexample. End with a short SHINE example contrasting direct adapter generation with iterative parameter updates. SHINE does not instantiate the DWS construction; keep the MLP expressivity results separate from the empirical claims about LLM adaptation.

Selection priority 8 · Topic 12Two academic hours

Online continual learning: stability, plasticity and replay

Representations, world models & continual learning

A major learning problem with a strong replay/Fisher derivation and an important distinction between forgetting and losing plasticity.

How can a network keep adapting to a changing data stream while retaining useful knowledge?

Scope for one session

Two academic hours: derive OCAR’s replay-constrained update, inspect one streaming experiment, and use CLA to connect prediction preservation with self-supervised representation alignment. A one-pass incoming stream can allow replay and bounded repeated updates on the current minibatch; it does not imply exactly one gradient step per observation.

Background & foundations

Chaudhry et al., On Tiny Episodic Memories in Continual Learning (2019): experience replay with limited memory. Kirkpatrick et al., Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting in Neural Networks (PNAS 2017): elastic weight consolidation and Fisher-weighted protection. Optional mathematical background: Martens & Grosse, Optimizing Neural Networks with Kronecker-factored Approximate Curvature (ICML 2015), selected sections introducing the Fisher and one K-FAC block.

Main paper

Urettini & Carta, Online Curvature-Aware Replay: Leveraging 2nd Order Information for Online Continual Learning (ICML 2025). Focus on Sections 3–5: formulate the streaming update, constrain changes to predictions on replay data, and derive the curvature-aware step.

Recent development & discussion

Cignoni et al., CLA: Latent Alignment for Online Continual Self-Supervised Learning (CoLLAs 2026): compare representation alignment with labeled prediction constraints. Conceptual companion: Dohare et al., Loss of Plasticity in Deep Continual Learning (Nature 2024), which distinguishes forgetting old knowledge from losing the ability to learn new tasks; its broader continual-learning experiments are not all single-pass online protocols.

Presentation focus

Derive the local KL/Fisher constraint and the preconditioned update on a small example. Explain damping, memory representativeness and the difference between forgetting and loss of plasticity. Evaluate accuracy during the stream as well as at its end. Check what information each experiment provides: OCAR hides task boundaries, but its class-incremental replay-Fisher weighting uses the number of new classes per experience.

Selection priority 9 · Topic 17Two academic hours

One-step generative models: Drifting and IMLE

Generative models

A fresh sample-matching view of generative modeling, especially useful when flow matching was already covered in the base course.

How can matching real and generated samples train a generator that produces a sample in one pass?

Scope for one session

One comparative seminar of two academic hours: derive Drifting as the central sample-matching mechanism, then compare IMLE’s nearest-neighbor assignments and ROMS-IMLE’s practical changes.

Background & foundations

Yujia Li, Swersky & Zemel, Generative Moment Matching Networks (ICML 2015), selected kernel/MMD material. Ke Li & Jitendra Malik, Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (2018), the data-to-generated nearest-neighbor assignment and regression algorithm.

Main paper

Deng, He Li, Tianhong Li, Du & He, Generative Modeling via Drifting (2026). Estimate an attraction-repulsion field from real and generated samples and fit frozen drifted targets.

Recent development & discussion

Chirag Vashist & Ke Li, ROMS-IMLE: A Minimalist Approach to Competitive Single-Step Generative Modelling (2026). Compare nearest-neighbor assignment, robust losses and intermediate-resolution supervision with the drifting update. Optional theory: Esteban-Casadevall et al., Kernel-Gradient Drifting Models (2026), including Max Welling. The authors’ ROMS-IMLE method page provides additional explanation and experiments.

Presentation focus

Introduce sample-based distribution matching, derive the drifting field and frozen-target regression, and study one toy experiment. Compare IMLE’s data-to-generated matching and ROMS-IMLE’s practical changes, including the direction of nearest-neighbor assignment, robust losses and intermediate-resolution supervision. Keep zero-drift and maximum-likelihood claims tied to their assumptions.

Selection priority 10 · Topic 05Two academic hours

Optimization at the edge of stability

Kernels, feature learning & optimization

A simple stability calculation leads to a surprising account of practical training dynamics, making this a particularly teachable theory seminar.

Why can training improve near the usual gradient-descent stability threshold?

Scope for one session

Two academic hours: one quadratic stability calculation, self-stabilization and the averaged dynamics of gradient descent.

Background & foundations

Quadratic losses, Hessian eigenvalues and the gradient-descent stability threshold 2 / learning rate. Cohen et al., Gradient Descent on Neural Networks Typically Occurs at the Edge of Stability (ICLR 2021), short-term loss oscillations and longer-term progress.

Main paper

Cohen, Damian, Talwalkar, Kolter & Lee, Understanding Optimization in Deep Learning with Central Flows (ICLR 2025). Start with the gradient-descent case and the authors’ illustrated explanation.

Recent development & discussion

Berthier, The Multiple Timescales of Gradient Descent on the Edge of Stability: A Perturbative Derivation of the Central Flow (2026), selected introduction and toy example. This formal asymptotic derivation does not provide remainder bounds or a convergence proof.

Presentation focus

Derive the quadratic stability threshold, examine self-stabilization, and show how averaging oscillations produces a smoother trajectory description. Use one example to compare the discrete iterates and central flow. Explain what this reveals about optimization dynamics without treating it as a general global-convergence theorem.

Selection priority 11 · Topic 13Two academic hours

Platonic representations and unpaired alignment

Representations, world models & continual learning

A strong hypothesis-driven discussion: shared representations, a constructive alignment algorithm and a critique of how similarity is measured.

Does shared representation geometry let us align independently trained models without paired alignment examples?

Scope for one session

Two academic hours: representation similarity, the Platonic hypothesis and one concrete unpaired alignment algorithm. Develop the Procrustes step on a small example; explain the full matching procedure without deriving every optimization subproblem.

Background & foundations

Kornblith et al., Similarity of Neural Network Representations Revisited (ICML 2019), centered kernel alignment. Huh et al., Position: The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (ICML 2024), the hypothesis and supporting experiments. Review an orthogonal Procrustes alignment using the SVD.

Main paper

Schnaus, Dagès, Cremers, Wang & Isola, Shared Geometry As A Rosetta Stone: Cross-Modal Alignment Without Paired Data (October 2026 preprint); the authors’ interactive project illustrates coarse cross-modal alignment without pairs at the alignment stage.

Recent development & discussion

Gröger, Wen & Brbic, Revisiting the Platonic Representation Hypothesis: An Aristotelian View (ICML 2026). Use the critique to examine which definitions and comparisons support claims about shared representations.

Presentation focus

Treat Rosetta as a practical test of a consequence of the Platonic hypothesis. Explain its coarse geometric initialization and alternating assignment / Procrustes updates, then examine the broad semantic structure it recovers and the finer details it misses. Paired samples are still used for evaluation and the CKA analysis of when alignment succeeds. Distinguish useful geometric correspondence from universal convergence or canonical features.

Selection priority 12 · Topic 22Two academic hours

GNNs: message passing and graph transformers

Graphs, symmetry & neural architectures

Adds graph and mathematical diversity; one graph counterexample makes the expressivity question concrete before modern transformer constructions.

What graph structure can a neural network distinguish, and what does global attention add?

Scope for one session

Two academic hours: message-passing expressivity, a concrete 1-WL limitation, and structural encodings in graph transformers.

Background & foundations

Sanchez-Lengeling et al., A Gentle Introduction to Graph Neural Networks (Distill 2021), a visual primer. Xu, Hu, Leskovec & Jegelka, How Powerful are Graph Neural Networks? (ICLR 2019), multiset aggregation, GIN and the 1-Weisfeiler-Leman expressivity ceiling.

Main paper

Stoll, Müller & Morris, Generalizable Insights for Graph Transformers in Theory and Practice (NeurIPS 2025), selected theory sections and one structural-encoding ablation.

Recent development & discussion

Ma et al., Plain Transformers Can be Powerful Graph Learners (TMLR 2026; 2025 preprint). Examine the graph-specific adaptations behind the title. Optional benchmark discussion: Bechler-Speicher et al., Position: Graph Learning Will Lose Relevance Due To Poor Benchmarks (ICML 2025).

Presentation focus

Work through a pair of graphs that 1-WL-style message passing cannot distinguish. Explain how structural or positional information changes the comparison with a graph transformer. Distinguish theoretical expressivity, measured accuracy and benchmark quality; leave the full higher-order hierarchy for optional reading.

μP, scaling and hyperparameter transfer

Kernels, feature learning & optimization

A practical scaling question with substantial theory: distinguish useful feature-learning parameterizations from merely making a network wider.

How should initialization and updates scale as networks become wider or deeper?

Background & foundations

Yang & Hu, Tensor Programs IV: Feature Learning in Infinite-Width Neural Networks (2021), selected sections on parameterization and feature learning. Review the NTK linearized regime as contrasting background; a separate NTK seminar is optional.

Main paper

Yang et al., Tensor Programs V: Tuning Large Neural Networks via Zero-Shot Hyperparameter Transfer (NeurIPS 2021; arXiv 2022).

Recent development & discussion

Dey et al., Don't Be Lazy: CompleteP Enables Compute-Efficient Deep Transformers (2025). Selection priority 15 · Topic 04Two academic hours

Why training works: convergence and loss landscapes

Kernels, feature learning & optimization

An important overarching question, but the restricted convergence proofs make this especially dependent on a strong presenter or instructor support.

Which assumptions let us prove that training reaches a global minimum, and how close are those proofs to practical networks?

Scope for one session

Two academic hours: derive PL-based loss decay and examine one trajectory-wise convergence argument for a restricted neural-network model. Concentrate on the assumptions that make the proof work.

Background & foundations

Karimi, Nutini & Schmidt, Linear Convergence of Gradient and Proximal-Gradient Methods Under the Polyak-Lojasiewicz Condition (2016), the PL inequality and gradient-flow loss decay. Historical context: Choromanska et al., The Loss Surfaces of Multilayer Networks (2015; 2014 preprint), the spin-glass analogy and its assumptions. Optional contrast: Soudry & Hoffer, Exponentially vanishing sub-optimal local minima in multilayer neural networks (ICLR 2018), volumes of regions containing minima versus a training trajectory.

Main paper

Dana, Pillaud-Vivien & Bach, Convergence of Shallow ReLU Networks on Weakly Interacting Data (NeurIPS 2025). Gradient flow in finite shallow ReLU networks, weakly interacting inputs and a particular asymmetric randomized initialization.

Recent development & discussion

Petit, Poon & Peyré, On the global convergence of gradient flow for wide shallow models beyond homogeneous nonlinearities (2026). The result is conditional: if the studied Wasserstein gradient flow converges, its limit is globally optimal. Watch list: Soudry’s publication page lists Global Convergence in Deep Networks via the Second Law of Thermodynamics (NeurIPS 2026); a linked manuscript was unavailable when this list was checked, so reading and claims remain TBD.

Presentation focus

Derive loss decay from a PL inequality, then explain how the main paper maintains a trajectory-wise condition. Inspect its data, initialization, width and gradient-flow assumptions. Separate landscape geometry, reaching a minimum, selecting a solution and generalization. These results make concrete progress; they do not supply a general guarantee for finite deep networks trained with SGD or Adam.

Selection priority 16 · Topic 15Two academic hours

Model merging and pretrained weight neighborhoods

Weight space & interpretability

Engaging and experimentally accessible; carefully distinguish task-vector addition, weight averaging and random-neighborhood prediction ensembles.

Which useful capabilities lie near a pretrained checkpoint, and how can we recover or combine them?

Scope for one session

Two academic hours: introduce task vectors and weight averaging, then center the seminar on Neural Thickets and the difference between combining weights and ensembling predictions.

Background & foundations

Ilharco et al., Editing Models with Task Arithmetic (2023), task vectors relative to a shared pretrained checkpoint. Wortsman et al., Model soups: averaging weights of multiple fine-tuned models improves accuracy without increasing inference time (ICML 2022), weight averaging versus prediction ensembles. Selected theory: Ortiz-Jimenez, Favero & Frossard, Task Arithmetic in the Tangent Space: Improved Editing of Pre-Trained Models (2023), the linearized model and weight disentanglement.

Main paper

Gan & Isola, Neural Thickets: Diverse Task Experts Are Dense Around Pretrained Weights (ICML 2026). RandOpt samples Gaussian weight perturbations, evaluates candidates on task data, selects experts and ensembles their predictions. See the authors’ illustrated project.

Recent development & discussion

Cendra et al., Can Bayesian Optimization Efficiently Find a Strong Single Expert in Neural Thickets? (2026), a follow-up in low-dimensional search settings. Empirical comparison: Hitit, Girrbach & Akata, A Systematic Study of In-the-Wild Model Merging for Large Language Models (TMLR 2026; 2025 preprint), heterogeneous checkpoints and conflicting objectives.

Presentation focus

Use function-space linearization to explain the intuition behind task-vector addition. Then develop expert density and diversity around a pretrained checkpoint, including the probability of finding a useful perturbation after repeated sampling. Compare weight averaging, expert selection and prediction ensembling, accounting for task-data evaluations and inference cost. These neighborhood results do not establish that random initialization or unrestricted gradient descent succeeds.

Symmetry, equivariance and canonicalization

Graphs, symmetry & neural architectures

Mathematically rich, but the continuity and topology result is a specialist choice with substantial prerequisite demands.

Can we enforce symmetry by putting each input into a canonical orientation?

Scope for one session

Advanced mathematical candidate. A concrete rotation or permutation example is enough to motivate the main result within two academic hours.

Background & foundations

Cohen & Welling, Group Equivariant Convolutional Networks (ICML 2016). Group actions, equivariance and weight sharing.

Main paper

Dym, Lawrence & Siegel, Equivariant Frames and the Impossibility of Continuous Canonicalization (ICML 2024).

Recent development & discussion

Shumaylov et al., Lie Algebra Canonicalization: Equivariant Neural Operators under arbitrary Lie Groups (ICLR 2025; 2024 preprint). Selected introduction and one application.

Presentation focus

Start with a group-action example, show why a canonical choice can become discontinuous under the stated assumptions, and explain how weighted frames address continuity. Leave general topology proofs and non-compact Lie-group machinery for optional reading.

Selection priority 20 · Topic 18Two academic hours

Diffusion language models

Generative models

Worthwhile discrete-generation material; its added value depends on how much diffusion and denoising students already studied.

How can denoising generate discrete text, and what changes when generation need not proceed left to right?

Background & foundations

Austin et al., Structured Denoising Diffusion Models in Discrete State-Spaces (2021), selected sections on transition matrices and absorbing-mask corruption.

Main paper

Sahoo et al., Simple and Effective Masked Diffusion Language Models (2024).

Recent development & discussion

Nie et al., Large Language Diffusion Models (LLaDA, 2025).

Presentation focus

Derive the masked-denoising objective and trace one sampling schedule, then compare refinement, parallelism and likelihood evaluation with autoregressive generation. The topic requires discrete corruption and language-specific evaluation beyond a continuous diffusion refresher.

Selection priority 21 · Topic 23Two academic hours

GNNs: over-squashing and graph geometry

Graphs, symmetry & neural architectures

A strong graph subject that can supply a short bottleneck comparison within the higher-priority graph seminar when slots are limited.

How does graph connectivity limit the information a message-passing network can transmit?

Scope for one session

Two academic hours: a bottleneck example, over-squashing and one graph-propagation construction. Introduce expander connectivity intuitively; full group-theory and spectral proofs are further reading.

Background & foundations

Kipf & Welling, Semi-Supervised Classification with Graph Convolutional Networks (ICLR 2017), neighborhood propagation. Alon & Yahav, On the Bottleneck of Graph Neural Networks and its Practical Implications (ICLR 2021), information bottlenecks and over-squashing.

Main paper

Wilson, Bechler-Speicher & Veličković, Cayley Graph Propagation (2025), an expander-based propagation template.

Recent development & discussion

Liang et al., Mitigating Over-Squashing in Graph Neural Networks by Spectrum-Preserving Sparsification (ICML 2025), a comparison of graph modification and propagation costs.

Presentation focus

Use a bottleneck graph to distinguish over-squashing from over-smoothing and from limited graph-distinguishing expressivity. Explain expander connectivity, inspect one propagation experiment, and account for the cost of modifying graph connectivity.

Selection priority 22 · Topic 21Two academic hours

KANs: learnable functions and scientific discovery

Graphs, symmetry & neural architectures

A useful critical architecture-comparison seminar, particularly for scientific-discovery interests, with less priority for the general course core.

When does learning the functions on network edges help more than learning ordinary weights?

Scope for one session

Two academic hours: the original architecture, one scientific example and one controlled comparison. Approximation-rate proofs are optional.

Background & foundations

Kolmogorov, On the Representation of Continuous Functions of Several Variables as Superpositions of Continuous Functions of One Variable and Addition (1957), theorem statement only. Review one-dimensional spline bases using the main paper’s architecture section. Separate representation existence from efficient approximation and learnability.

Main paper

Liu et al., KAN: Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (ICLR 2025; 2024 preprint), selected sections on learned edge functions, grid refinement and one function-fitting experiment.

Recent development & discussion

Liu et al., KAN 2.0: Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks Meet Science (2024), one scientific example. Essential comparison: Yu, Yu & Wang, KAN or MLP: A Fairer Comparison (2024). Recent optional theory: Kratsios, Kim & Furuya, Approximation Rates in Besov Norms and Sample-Complexity of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks with Residual Connections (2025), assumptions and result statements.

Presentation focus

Build a small spline-based KAN and explain its compositional bias and possible formula recovery. Compare against an MLP with matched parameters or FLOPs and a spline-equipped MLP. Ask which benefit comes from the function basis and which comes from the architecture; the representation theorem alone does not establish a practical advantage.

Selection priority 23 · Topic 20Two academic hours

Oscillatory neurons and synchronization

Graphs, symmetry & neural architectures

An attractive exploratory choice connecting dynamics and computation; select it as a deliberate physics-oriented wildcard.

Can neural representations bind information through synchronization?

Scope for one session

Exploratory candidate connecting neural computation with dynamical systems, including work by Max Welling.

Background & foundations

Strogatz, From Kuramoto to Crawford: exploring the onset of synchronization in populations of coupled oscillators (2000). Sections 2 and 3.1–3.3 on coupled phases and synchronization (author-hosted PDF).

Main paper

Miyato, Löwe, Geiger & Welling, Artificial Kuramoto Oscillatory Neurons (ICLR 2025; 2024 preprint).

Recent development & discussion

Iqbal et al., Spontaneous symmetry breaking and Goldstone modes for deep information propagation (2026 preprint). A related perspective on symmetry and persistent information propagation, rather than a direct analysis of AKOrN.

Presentation focus

Derive the two-oscillator intuition, trace the generalized neural update, and study one object-discovery or reasoning experiment. Use the Goldstone paper for a brief connection to depth and memory; its physics derivations are optional.
