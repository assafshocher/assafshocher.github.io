Schedule & readings

Learn the foundations, understand a central paper, examine what came next.

This is the working 13-week program. Each reading bundle includes background, a main paper, and recent developments or discussion resources. When the main paper is itself recent, its companion can be a theoretical analysis or practical resource. Selected sections will be assigned; students are not expected to read every linked paper in full.

TTT has two sessions: adaptation under distribution shift, including the original Sun et al. paper and IT³; and learning as sequence memory. Drifting models and IMLE each have a dedicated generative-modeling session. Diffusion language models, Muon and RL reasoning remain available as optional substitutions. Flow matching is assumed background from the base course.

Part 01

Kernels, feature learning & scaling

Week 01Presenter: TBD

NTK and feature learning

When can a neural network be understood as a kernel method, and when do its features change?

Background & foundations

Lee et al., Wide Neural Networks of Any Depth Evolve as Linear Models Under Gradient Descent (2019), selected sections on Taylor linearization and squared-loss dynamics. Review Jacobians, Gram matrices and gradient flow.

Main paper

Jacot, Gabriel & Hongler, Neural Tangent Kernel: Convergence and Generalization in Neural Networks (2018).

Recent development & discussion

Haas et al., On the Surprising Effectiveness of Large Learning Rates under Standard Width Scaling (2025).

Presentation focus

Derive function-space gradient flow and its solution for squared loss, then compare a finite network with its linearization. Use the recent paper to examine why width, parameterization, learning rate and the choice of loss matter for claims about feature learning.

μP, scaling and hyperparameter transfer

How should initialization and updates scale as networks become wider or deeper?

Background & foundations

Yang & Hu, Tensor Programs IV: Feature Learning in Infinite-Width Neural Networks (2021), selected sections on parameterization and feature learning. The preceding NTK session supplies the contrasting linearized regime.

Main paper

Yang et al., Tensor Programs V: Tuning Large Neural Networks via Zero-Shot Hyperparameter Transfer (NeurIPS 2021; arXiv 2022).

Recent development & discussion

Dey et al., Don't Be Lazy: CompleteP Enables Compute-Efficient Deep Transformers (2025).

Presentation focus

Work through activation and update scaling in a small multilayer network, and inspect a learning-rate transfer experiment. Discuss the additional constraints introduced by depth; width transfer alone does not establish depth transfer or effective nonlinear learning.

Part 02

Sequence models & learning at test time

Week 03Presenter: TBD

State-space models and Mamba

How do recurrence, selectivity and structured matrices produce an efficient sequence model?

Background & foundations

Gu, Goel & Ré, Efficiently Modeling Long Sequences with Structured State Spaces (S4, 2021/22), selected sections on discretization and recurrence. Gu & Dao, Mamba: Linear-Time Sequence Modeling with Selective State Spaces (2023), selected sections on input-dependent dynamics.

Main paper

Dao & Gu, Transformers Are SSMs: Generalized Models and Efficient Algorithms Through Structured State Space Duality (Mamba-2, 2024).

Recent development & discussion

Lahoti et al., Mamba-3: Improved Sequence Modeling Using State Space Principles (2026).

Presentation focus

Unroll an SSM into a structured matrix and explain the restricted attention–SSM duality used by Mamba-2. Examine a state-tracking experiment from Mamba-3 and distinguish algorithmic complexity from measured hardware efficiency.

In-context learning as an algorithm

How can a forward pass perform learning without changing the network's parameters?

Background & foundations

von Oswald et al., Transformers Learn In-Context by Gradient Descent (2023), selected sections on the linear-attention construction. Review least squares, ridge regression and Bayesian prediction for linear models.

Main paper

Akyürek et al., What Learning Algorithm Is In-Context Learning? Investigations with Linear Models (2023).

Recent development & discussion

Wurgaft et al., In-Context Learning Strategies Emerge Rationally (2025).

Presentation focus

Work through one regression-learning construction and compare gradient-descent, ridge and Bayesian predictors on a controlled task distribution. Separate existence constructions and observed behavior in trained synthetic-task models from claims about general language models.

Week 05Presenter: TBD

Test-time training: adaptation and IT³

What unlabeled objective makes adaptation improve the task we actually care about?

Background & foundations

Sun, Wang, Liu, Miller, Efros & Hardt, Test-Time Training with Self-Supervision for Generalization under Distribution Shifts (ICML 2020). Shared encoders, self-supervised auxiliary tasks and the gradient-alignment condition under the paper’s smooth/convex assumptions.

Main paper

Durasov, Shocher, Oner, Chechik, Efros & Fua, IT³: Idempotent Test-Time Training (ICML 2025). Learn prediction feedback and use consistency between successive predictions as the test-time objective.

Recent development & discussion

IT³ is the recent anchor. Supplementary resources: arXiv paper and official implementation. Compare its idempotence-based consistency objective with the original TTT auxiliary-task design.

Presentation focus

Derive the original gradient-alignment argument and IT³ consistency loss, then inspect an adaptation experiment and a failure case. IT³ requires its own training setup; discuss when consistency can be uninformative and how to stop adaptation.

Week 06Presenter: TBD

Test-time training: learning as neural memory

How can parameter updates store context, and how should training prepare a model to learn at inference?

Background & foundations

Ba et al., Using Fast Weights to Attend to the Recent Past (2016). Fast versus slow weights and associative memory; recap the original Sun et al. TTT formulation.

Main paper

Sun et al., Learning to (Learn at Test Time): RNNs with Expressive Hidden States (2024). A model is the recurrent hidden state, and self-supervised gradient updates are the state transition.

Recent development & discussion

Tandon et al., End-to-End Test-Time Training for Long Context (2025). Next-token updates compress context into weights, with a meta-learned initialization.

Presentation focus

Work through a TTT-Linear update and its recurrent computation. Compare layer-local reconstruction with end-to-end next-token adaptation; distinguish sequence memory from adapting to a shifted test distribution.

Hypernetworks and generated adaptation

Can a network generate the parameters another network needs for a new task or context?

Background & foundations

Ha, Dai & Le, HyperNetworks (2016/17). Hu et al., LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models (2021/22), selected sections on low-rank parameter updates.

Main paper

Charakorn et al., Text-to-LoRA: Instant Transformer Adaption (2025).

Recent development & discussion

Charakorn et al., Doc-to-LoRA: Learning to Instantly Internalize Contexts (2026).

Presentation focus

Explain the conditioning signal, generated low-rank update and training objective, then examine a held-out-task or document experiment. Compare the cost and information limits of generated adaptation with fine-tuning and keeping the original context available.

JEPA and predictive world models

When does prediction in representation space support understanding, dynamics and planning?

Background & foundations

Assran et al., Self-Supervised Learning from Images with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA, 2023), selected sections on masking, target representations and collapse prevention.

Main paper

Assran et al., V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised Video Models Enable Understanding, Prediction and Planning (2025).

Maes et al., LeWorldModel: Stable End-to-End Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture from Pixels (2026).

Presentation focus

Trace the representation-prediction objective and action-conditioned planning loop, then analyze a planning result and its data requirements. Contrast target-network stabilization with regularizing the latent distribution; representation quality and accurate action-conditioned dynamics are separate questions.

Model merging and task arithmetic

Why can adding parameter updates combine capabilities, and when does task interference prevent it?

Background & foundations

Ilharco et al., Editing Models with Task Arithmetic (2023). Review parameter differences relative to a shared pretrained checkpoint and the NTK session's function-space linearization.

Main paper

Ortiz-Jimenez, Favero & Frossard, Task Arithmetic in the Tangent Space: Improved Editing of Pre-Trained Models (2023).

Recent development & discussion

Hitit, Girrbach & Akata, A Systematic Study of In-the-Wild Model Merging for Large Language Models (2025 preprint; TMLR 2026).

Presentation focus

Derive how adding task vectors affects a linearized model and examine the proposed weight-disentanglement mechanism. Compare controlled task combinations with heterogeneous checkpoints, including conflicting objectives and gains relative to the best individual model.

Part 05

New generative frameworks

Week 12Presenter: TBD

Drifting models: training-time distribution dynamics

How can attraction and repulsion during training produce a one-pass generator, and when does zero drift identify the correct distribution?

Background & foundations

Yujia Li, Swersky & Zemel, Generative Moment Matching Networks (ICML 2015). Kernel mean embeddings, maximum mean discrepancy and distribution matching from samples.

Main paper

Deng, He Li, Tianhong Li, Du & He, Generative Modeling via Drifting (2026). Estimate an attraction-repulsion field from real and generated samples and fit frozen drifted targets.

Recent development & discussion

Esteban-Casadevall et al., Kernel-Gradient Drifting Models (2026), including Max Welling. A mathematical companion for kernel-gradient fields, smoothing and distribution identifiability. Original drifting project and demonstrations.

Presentation focus

Derive the drift and regression update, including the role of stopping gradients through the target. Examine a 2D example, equilibrium assumptions and one ablation; arbitrary zero drift need not certify distribution equality.

Week 13Presenter: TBD

IMLE and one-step generative models

Can data-to-generated nearest-neighbor matching achieve coverage and high fidelity without an iterative sampler?

Background & foundations

Ke Li & Jitendra Malik, Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (2018). Implicit distributions, nearest-neighbor assignment, mode coverage and the likelihood connection under stated assumptions.

Main paper

Chirag Vashist & Ke Li, ROMS-IMLE: A Minimalist Approach to Competitive Single-Step Generative Modelling (2026). Revisit IMLE with intermediate-resolution supervision and robust losses.

Recent development & discussion

ROMS-IMLE is the recent anchor. The authors’ method page provides additional explanation and experiments.

Presentation focus

Explain the direction of nearest-neighbor matching, latent assignment and alternating regression, then examine sampling cost and the ROMS-IMLE ablations. Keep the maximum-likelihood connection tied to its assumptions.

Diffusion language models

How can denoising generate discrete text, and what changes when generation need not proceed left to right?

Background & foundations

Austin et al., Structured Denoising Diffusion Models in Discrete State-Spaces (2021), selected sections on transition matrices and absorbing-mask corruption.

Main paper

Sahoo et al., Simple and Effective Masked Diffusion Language Models (2024).

Recent development & discussion

Nie et al., Large Language Diffusion Models (LLaDA, 2025).

Presentation focus

Derive the masked-denoising objective and trace one sampling schedule, then compare refinement, parallelism and likelihood evaluation with autoregressive generation. The topic requires discrete corruption and language-specific evaluation beyond a continuous diffusion refresher.

Optional topic

Geometry of optimization: Muon

How does the norm used to measure an update change the direction of steepest descent?

Background & foundations

Bernstein & Newhouse, Old Optimizer, New Norm: An Anthology (2024), selected sections on steepest descent, dual norms and matrix operator norms.

Main paper

Liu et al., Muon Is Scalable for LLM Training (2025).

Recent development & discussion

Kovalev, Understanding Gradient Orthogonalization for Deep Learning via Non-Euclidean Trust-Region Optimization (2025).

Presentation focus

Derive the spectral-norm-constrained linearized update using the SVD and explain its polar factor and Newton–Schulz approximation. Examine a scaling experiment and distinguish the geometric argument from implementation choices, weight decay and empirical comparisons with AdamW.

Optional topic

RL for reasoning with verifiable rewards

Does reward-based training discover successful reasoning behavior or change which existing behavior is sampled?

Background & foundations

Spinning Up: Part 3—Introduction to Policy Optimization, selected sections on the log-derivative policy gradient and baselines. Reserve 25–30 minutes to introduce policies, sampled completions, rewards and group-relative advantages; prior RL coursework is not assumed.

Main paper

DeepSeek-AI et al., DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning (2025), focusing on R1-Zero and the learning objective.

Recent development & discussion

Yue et al., Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model? (2025). Counterpoint: Liu et al., ProRL: Prolonged Reinforcement Learning Expands Reasoning Boundaries in Large Language Models (2025).

Presentation focus

Derive the policy-gradient identity before introducing group-relative advantages, clipping and KL regularization. Compare pass@k evidence under the papers' different training and evaluation settings; finite sampling cannot establish that a base model assigns zero probability to a solution.
